## 2. Campaign Context & The Fellowship

This adventure module seamlessly builds upon the events of the campaign chronicle recorded in `campaign_log.md` and prepared in `session_prep_armouries.md`.

### 2.1 The Strategic Stakes
Lord Balin has arrived in person at the East-Gate Camp in Dimrill Dale, accompanied by his niece Commander Fróra and a small vanguard of veteran Longbeards. While the Company’s earlier victories (clearing the First Hall, seizing Durin’s Bridge, driving off Grimnar, and securing Thrym Thistlebeard’s Caves) have established a vital foothold, Balin's total force numbers barely fifty Dwarves. 

**King Dáin Ironfoot** remains in Erebor, deeply cautious of the nameless dread that lingers in the roots of the mountain. He refuses to commit the armies and treasury of the Lonely Mountain without undeniable proof that the treasures of Khazad-dûm can be reclaimed and that Balin’s colony can sustain itself.

To equip Balin’s vanguard and send back irrefutable proof to King Dáin, the Company must plunge into the deeps and plunder **The Armouries of the Third Deep**—the ancient subterranean arsenal where the legendary Tunnel-guards were garrisoned and where the fabled **Axe of Durin** is rumoured to rest.

### 3.1 The Delving of the Third Deep
Constructed in the First Age following the death of Durin I, the Upper Deeps were designed as an impenetrable subterranean fortress and weapons manufactory. The Dwarves feared that Morgoth's conquering hosts might sweep over Eriador and lay siege to Khazad-dûm.

The Armouries were built to equip a host of twenty thousand Dwarven warriors in full gromril-mail and rune-carved steel. Within these halls stood the barracks and drill-yards of the **Tunnel-guards**—an elite order of Dwarven champions trained specifically in blind-fighting, subterranean chokepoints, and subterranean breaching warfare.

### 3.2 The Disaster of the Poisoned Halls (1980 T.A.)
When **Durin’s Bane** arose from the abyssal mines below the Ninth Deep, the Tunnel-guards were the first organized martial force to counter-attack. The Dwarven Marshal rallied his elite knights in the Twelfth and Fourteenth Halls of the Second/Third Deep, attempting to seal the blast-doors and contain the terror.

The Balrog did not merely fight with sword and whip of flame; it exhaled a terrible subterranean miasma—a heavy, sulfurous neurotoxin known as **The Breath of the Pit**. The Tunnel-guards perished where they stood, suffocating in their hermetically sealed armor. Because the toxic gas settled in the lower galleries and never dissipated, the fallen Dwarves and their armaments remain miraculously preserved, untainted by rot, vermin, or scavengers for a thousand years.

### 3.3 The Present Situation (2989 T.A.)
The Armouries are currently a fractured, perilous buffer zone:
1. **The Upper Sections (Mustering-Yard & Gatehouses)**: Controlled intermittently by patrols from **Malech One-eye** (Lord of the Dwarrowdelf) and emissaries from **Thu the Firespeaker** (High Priest of the Udûn Balrog Cult).
2. **The Middle Wings (Broken Hall & Second Armoury)**: Despoiled and littered with crude Orc traps and defiled murals.
3. **The Poisoned Halls & Upper Armoury**: Unlooted due to the lethal gas. Goblins who ventured inside died immediately, leaving their withered corpses at the threshold.
4. **The Hall of the Mauler**: Dominated by **The Mauler**—a colossal Great Cave-troll that fled the slaughter of Azanulbizar, driven half-mad by pain, now obsessive about strapping every piece of Dwarven ironmongery to its hide.
5. **The Lower Armoury (The Vault)**: Remains sealed behind **The King’s Door**, an adamantine portal requiring two masterwork keys: the *King's Key* (lost in the Last Redoubt of Old Moria) and the *Marshal's Key* (carried into battle and stolen by deep goblins).

                                       |
                                       v
